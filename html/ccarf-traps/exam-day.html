Exam day

The exam is closed-book, on one screen, with no AI help, so you bring a method rather than a pile of facts. This page covers the format, the pace, the reading method, what the official samples teach, and the logistics that end sittings before they start.

The format

60
items in 4 scenarios, with several items hanging off each scenario
120
minutes (2 per item); plan ~135 min of seat time
720
scaled pass, 100–1,000, criterion-referenced (not a curve)
2
item types; each item states how many responses to select

Multiple-response trap: an earlier version of the guide said every item had one right answer and three distractors, and all the published samples are single-answer. The current exam also has multiple-response items. An item asking for two is asking two questions: judge each option on its own, and make sure your pair isn't one idea in two forms.

Pace: two passes

Item 20by 0:30
Item 40by 1:00
Item 60by 1:30, end of pass 1
30 minpass 2: flagged items

Guess, flag, finish, return. Never leave a blank, because a blank can't be marked correct. Read each scenario once, build a picture of it (its tools, its targets, where the human sits), then let the items walk through it.

Reading method

  1. Last sentence first

    Find the ask: first step, root cause, most maintainable, most reliable. That word decides between a cheap fix and a mechanism.

  2. Find the cause the stem handed you

    Stems state the diagnosis mid-prose (“both descriptions are one line”, “the file lives in her home directory”). Any option fixing some other weakness is a distractor.

  3. Find the clause that limits valid answers

    An absolute (must / never / always / cannot) or a consequence that acts like one. “Must apply automatically” rules out anything a person has to invoke.

  4. Kill by shape, not option by option

    Plausible advice aimed past the cause · the right answer to a different question · more machinery than the cause needs. See Trap Families.

  5. Count the responses, then move on

    Nothing carries between items. Few-shot examples can be wrong on two items and right on the third, because the requirement moved while the system stayed the same.
